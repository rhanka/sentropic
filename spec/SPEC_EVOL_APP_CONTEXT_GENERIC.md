## 1. Résumé pour l'owner (FR)

**Le modèle générique, en mots simples.** Chaque application (immo d'abord, puis n'importe quelle autre) obtient une « carte » de son propre contexte, construite automatiquement à un commit figé : ses specs et exigences, ses items et décisions Track, son modèle de données, ses écrans, ses composants d'interface, ses endpoints d'API et le code qui les relie. Cette carte est un graphe typé par les ontologies d'engram, sans aucun lien deviné : un lien existe seulement s'il est déclaré ou lu dans un arbre syntaxique, sinon le système émet un diagnostic explicite. La carte est servie par MCP, en lecture, avec un budget de tokens strict et une réponse toujours rattachée au commit indexé — jamais à un autre.

**Ce que le tour 3 corrige, après trois revues indépendantes de la v2.** Le principe posé en v2 reste : **aucune lecture n'existe sans session admise** — h2a admet chaque session avant son démarrage (même contrat au lancement CLI et au lancement MCP), consigne son périmètre effectif dans un manifeste protégé, jamais modifiable par l'agent, et l'affiche avant le travail ; cluster-mesh rattache chaque connexion MCP à cette session précise. Le tour 3 ferme quatre failles que les revues ont démontrées : une section de spec autorisée ne peut plus laisser fuir le texte d'une sous-section interdite (toute plage de texte mélangée est refusée entière, extraits compris) ; une condition non résolue refuse désormais avant toute autorisation qui la recouvre, niveau par niveau ; le périmètre sait désigner précisément une section, une exigence ou un élément Track, sans autoriser leurs voisins ; et la création d'un nouveau fichier dans le périmètre a enfin une règle — chaque création ou renommage est jugé au moment de l'écriture, pas seulement les fichiers déjà présents au commit. Deux sessions du même agent, même sur le même lot de travail, peuvent porter des périmètres différents, et chaque session ne peut interroger que le commit pour lequel elle a été admise.

**Ce que le tour 4 précise, après les relectures de la v3 (engram : approuvé, inchangé).** Une liste d'autorisation explicitement vide refuse désormais avant toute condition vraie qui la recouvrirait, et les règles de chemins retenues dans le manifeste sont séparées en lecture et en écriture, source par source, avec l'état figé de chaque condition — une source qui ne contraint que l'écriture, comme le registre immo, s'abstient en lecture. Le registre immo suit une règle de moment unique : extraction statique au build du graphe (dépôt, chemin, commit complet et empreinte consignés), l'admission vérifie seulement que le build a utilisé ce même commit et cette même empreinte. Côté builder, l'arbre protégé n'est jamais inscriptible directement par un programme (`exec`/`pty` inclus) : les écritures vivent dans un espace jetable et seul un passage contrôlé, qui juge chaque fichier avant toute mutation de la destination protégée, peut les y reporter ; tant que ce passage n'est pas qualifié, l'admission en isolation système est refusée. Chaque opération est enfin jugée sur la sélection finale des fichiers modifiables — un fichier existant non sélectionné reste refusé même sous des motifs larges — et une feuille de spec autorisée reste consultable même si sa section parente, elle, ne l'est pas.

**Qui possède quoi.** Engram possède le vocabulaire (les ontologies `archi` pour l'architecture des composants et `app-context@1` pour écrans, composants, endpoints, specs, Track et données) et la construction du graphe par application ; il applique les restrictions qu'on lui transmet et marque chaque élément servi `inScope` (modifiable) ou `readOnly` (lisible, non modifiable), sans accorder aucun droit. H2a possède l'admission : l'autorisation de lancer une session, le calcul du périmètre effectif et son rattachement à la session. Track décrit le travail : les lots (workpackages), leurs cibles applicatives par liens typés, leurs restrictions déclarées — sans pouvoir s'accorder de droits à lui-même. Cluster-mesh possède le registre des applications entre elles et l'exposition MCP authentifiée, par extension de l'enregistrement applicatif existant. L'application (immo, sentropic) ne fait que consommer.

**Le périmètre de développement explicite (devScope).** La session désigne un workpackage précis, éventuellement un item. Le périmètre effectif est l'**intersection**, calculée séparément pour la lecture et pour l'écriture, de la politique d'admission, des restrictions de toute la chaîne des workpackages et phases ancêtres, et de la demande de lancement ; les interdictions priment, une condition non résolue refuse, une liste explicitement vide n'autorise rien. Un préréglage (`ui`, `api`, `jobs`, `data`, `fullstack`) reste une étiquette pratique ; ce qui fait foi, ce sont les cibles lisibles et modifiables résolues — y compris, quand il le faut, une section de spec ou un élément Track précis — traduites en chemins au commit épinglé, plus les zones où la session peut créer de nouveaux fichiers. Toute modification de parenté, de sélection d'item ou de déclaration Track invalide la résolution ; toute extension demande une nouvelle admission ; une révocation prend effet avant l'appel suivant.

**Ce qui est livré d'abord, et ce qui attend sa preuve.** La première phase livre la **consultation autorisée** : lecture filtrée, traçable, par session. Elle ne promet aucun confinement des écritures. Une session en écriture n'est admise que si le contrôle local choisi par l'owner (§8, Q7) est réellement disponible et qualifié sur l'hôte — garde de développement qualifiée, ou isolation système côté builder (arbre protégé jamais inscriptible directement, canaux `exec`/`pty` inclus, écritures médiées depuis un espace jetable, §4.4 point 3b) ; sinon le lancement en écriture est refusé, ou explicitement limité à la consultation si l'appelant l'a demandé. Le contrôle de branche et la preuve Track constatent après coup ; ils ne sont jamais présentés comme un confinement.

**Ce qu'immo obtient.** Le graphe de contexte de `radar-immobilier` au commit admis, les cinq outils MCP en lecture filtrée via son MCP d'application, le raccordement de ses specs par le manifeste central `.engram/spec-map.yaml` sans réécrire les documents, et son modèle Drizzle comme source de vérité. Son plafond d'écriture n'est plus à choisir : c'est la décision déjà prise le 12 novembre — le fichier registre d'immo, **référencé** par dépôt, chemin, commit et empreinte, résolu par extraction statique au build du graphe et seulement vérifié à l'admission (même commit, même empreinte), jamais recopié dans cette spec ni ailleurs (§6.2.1). Ce plafond couvre l'interface et les familles de paquets applicatifs que le registre autorise ; il n'est pas limité à l'interface. L'admission l'intersecte ensuite avec la politique, les restrictions du lot de travail et la demande de session : une session donnée peut être plus étroite, jamais plus large. Le filtre « interface seule » subsiste comme simple vue d'architecture pour coder confortablement, sans aucune valeur d'autorisation.

**Ce que « vibe coding full stack » demandera plus tard, prévu dès maintenant.** Le même graphe couvre déjà endpoints, jobs et entités de données ; le même contrat de périmètre sait les nommer en lecture et en écriture. Élargir reviendra à admettre de nouvelles sessions sur des cibles `api`/`data`/`fullstack` explicites — jamais à traduire une demande en accès complet par défaut — sans changer ni les outils MCP, ni le registre, ni la forme des admissions.

**Décisions pour l'owner : une seule section, une seule passe (§8 « Décisions owner »).** Onze questions ouvertes, chacune avec ses options, sa recommandation et la conséquence de chaque option en une ligne : les sept questions de h2a/track (autorité du périmètre, représentation des cibles Track, héritage, évolution en session, liaison avec cluster-mesh, consultation depuis le périmètre immo, garantie locale), puis le véhicule du pont entre ontologies, le raccordement des specs immo, et le sort différé de Prisma et des faits DDL SQL. Trois points ne sont plus des questions et sont listés comme décisions acquises en tête de §8 : sans session admise, aucune lecture ; le marqueur YAML clôturé reste abandonné ; le plafond d'écriture immo est le fichier registre référencé (ancienne Q9, close). Budget de lecture annoncé en tête de §8.

**Coût recalculé.** 128 heures-agent (§7), en plus des 35 heures gelées côté engram et du programme builder étape 1. Les 112 heures de la v2 sont conservées comme base ; le tour 3 ajoute 16 heures dans les lots concernés pour le refus des plages de texte mélangées, les références précises aux specs et à Track, l'évaluation niveau par niveau, la résolution du plafond référencé immo, et le contrôle des créations et renommages à chaque écriture. Le tour 4 élargit les preuves de sortie des lots déjà chiffrés (règles par dimension, passage builder contrôlé, prédicats par opération, feuille propre) sans changer ce total (§0.1). Le premier jalon vérifiable reste local et sans hébergement (§7). Cartes de composants en texte : §3.6 ; processus en étapes BPMN texte : §5.5 ; glossaire court : §10.

## 3. Component-architecture model (generic, ontology-typed)

**D-G2 — Two ontologies, one declared bridge.** `[JUGEMENT]` The generic model reuses engram's two existing vocabularies at their native grain and adds nothing at either grain except the bridge (§3.4) and `DevScope` (§4.1):

- `archi@1` = system grain: what the app is made of as deployable/ownable parts (packages, runtimes, services, APIs, stores), authored per app as an instance document, validated by engram. Screens belong to this described application architecture; describing them in cluster-mesh does not imply cluster-mesh hosts their execution `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/h2a/tmp/h-cond/appctx-scope/NOTE-scope-sessions-wp.fr.md:28]`.
- `app-context@1` = code grain: what a coding session touches (routes/screens, UI components, endpoints, data entities/fields, code symbols) and what governs it (specs, requirements, Track), extracted per commit.

### 3.1 Entity types

**Code grain (`app-context@1`, frozen).** 12 app-context types `Spec, SpecSection, Requirement, TrackWorkpackage, TrackItem, TrackDecision, DataEntity, DataField, Route, Component, ApiEndpoint, CodeSymbol`, plus `File, Commit, Branch` and 5 MCP-config types `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:78]`. A screen is a `Route` (profile alias `screen`) `[FAIT: git dcdd0a37:profiles/app-context/v1/profile.yaml — Route aliases [screen, page route, ui route]]`; a UI component is `Component` keyed by `[repoRelPath, exportName]` `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:110]`.

**System grain (`archi@1`, existing).** Kinds and closed forms as in §2.1 `[FAIT: origin 1c0321e4:src/ontology-guardian/archi-profile.ts:38]`. Generic component architecture maps onto them without extension `[JUGEMENT]`:

| Architecture concern | archi@1 kind/form | app-context@1 counterpart |
|---|---|---|
| UI / screens | `package` form `app`, or `component` inside it | `Route`, `Component` |
| API surface | `api` forms `http` / `mcp` / `cli` | `ApiEndpoint`, `CodeSymbol --handles-->` |
| Jobs / workers | `runtime` form `job` (also `daemon`) | `CodeSymbol` entry points (no endpoint) |
| Data | `store` forms `database` / `object-store` / `file` / `jsonl` / `graph` (physical storage; in the `archi@2` target the conceptual business object stays a separate `dataEntity`) | `DataEntity`, `DataField` — bridged to `store` only (§3.4) |
| Libraries | `package` form `library` | `Component`, `CodeSymbol` by file |
| Long-running processes | `service`, `runtime` forms `mcp-server` / `daemon` | handler `CodeSymbol`s |
| Ownership/source | `repo`, relations `managedIn`, `ownedBy`, `partOf` `[FAIT: origin 1c0321e4:src/ontology-guardian/archi-profile.ts:53]` | `File`, `Commit` provenance |

**Declared target (cited, not pinned).** `archi@2` exists as a proposed specification with 6 layers and 21 kinds; its `store` is physical storage and its `dataEntity` is a conceptual business object, deliberately distinct kinds `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/archi-card-semantics/SPEC_EVOL_ARCHI_V2_ONTOLOGY.r4.md:36]`. The owner's mixed-view decision adds bridge profile `archi2-erd-bridge@1`: `storedIn` (erd entity → store, one store per entity, containment, lifting along `partOf`) and `realizes` (erd entity → dataEntity) `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/archi-card-semantics/BRIDGE-ARCHI-ERD.md:11]`. This design keeps `archi@1` pinned now and treats `@2` adoption as a versioned migration of instance documents (Q10), not a precondition. Instance documents are therefore authored so that stores and data entities stay expressible in both versions: nothing conceptual is modelled as a store, and no physical table is modelled as a business object (§6.2).

### 3.2 Relations (code grain, frozen subset used by the generic model)

All 30 relations are declared in the profile and checked extractor-by-extractor `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:115]`. The load-bearing ones for component architecture:

`Spec|SpecSection|Requirement --governs--> Route|Component|ApiEndpoint|DataEntity|DataField|CodeSymbol` (explicit declarations only) `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:123]`; `Route --renders--> Component` `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:130]`; `Component --uses--> Component`, `Component|CodeSymbol --calls--> ApiEndpoint` only when method and path are statically literal `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:340]`; `ApiEndpoint|CodeSymbol --reads|writes--> DataEntity|DataField` from statement type, never keyword presence `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/DESIGN-NOTE-EXTRACTORS-NO-GUESS.md:15]`; `TrackItem --in_workpackage--> TrackWorkpackage`, `TrackItem|TrackWorkpackage --tracks-->`, `TrackDecision --decides-->` from explicit ULIDs or explicit spec paths only `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:323]`. Multiple relations on one pair are preserved as separate lossless records `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:175]`.

So one UI component/screen is linked, in one commit-pinned graph, to: its governing spec/section/requirement (`governs`, with readable text spans `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:249]`), its Track items/WP/decisions (`tracks`, `decides`, `in_workpackage`), its data model (`calls → handles → reads|writes → has_field/references`), and its code paths (every node carries `file` + line-span provenance at the build commit) — which is exactly the owner's « spec / track liés dans l'ontologie, et modèle de donnée / écrans / composant aussi ». Read filtering (§5.2) prunes this linked view per session; it never rewrites the graph.

### 3.3 Identity and versioning

- Code-grain identity is structural and frozen: tuple-hashed ids `appContextNodeId(repoKey, nodeType, seedTuple)` → `spec:`/`comp:`/`ep:`/`entity:`… `<32-hex>` `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:82]`; every node/diagnostic id comes from the injective tuple builder, no ad-hoc concatenation `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/DESIGN-NOTE-EXTRACTORS-NO-GUESS.md:19]`. Identity is per-repo and per-profile-version (`"app-context@1"` is inside the hash input), so a profile major change re-keys deliberately. The same repo-relative path in two bound repos therefore keys two distinct nodes; nothing cross-repo is ever merged by path (§5.3 test).
- Graph versioning is the commit: `indexedCommit = graph.provenance.source_hash`; a graph is never answered for another commit `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/DESIGN-NOTE-APP-CONTEXT.md:17]`, and the profile is pinned by `profile_hash` checked before traversal (`profile-incompatible` otherwise) `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:385]`.
- `[JUGEMENT]` System-grain identity is `(tenantId, appInstanceId, archiNodeKey)` where `archiNodeKey` is the authored id inside the app's archi instance document. The document lives in the app repo, so its operative version at a context commit is the **content digest of the document as read at graph build for that commit**, recorded in the graph metadata by the bridge assembly (§3.4); the registration (§3.5) holds only the digest expected for its current revision. Instance documents are authored in the owning repo and ingested/validated by engram — the pattern already fixed for archi descriptions `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/archi-card-semantics/SPEC_EVOL_ARCHI_CARDS.r2.md:36]`. A bridge target that disappears at a new commit yields a diagnostic at build time, never a silent drop (§3.4).

### 3.4 The archi ↔ app-context bridge (declared, never inferred)

`[JUGEMENT]` **D-G3.** The bridge is an explicit declaration in the app repo, assembled at graph-build time into graph edges, so it is commit-pinned like everything else:

- Declaration file: `.engram/archi-bridge.yaml` (new, app-owned content), entries `{ archi: <archiNodeKey>, targets: [<TargetSelector>…] }` reusing the frozen `TargetSelector` union (`component|route|endpoint|entity|field|symbol`) `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:391]`.
- Assembly: `app-context-assemble` resolves each selector with the existing component-wise rules; zero/several candidates → `target-not-found`/`target-ambiguous` diagnostic and no edge `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:243]`. The assembly also stamps the graph with `{archiProfile, archiDocumentDigest}` for the build commit (§3.3).
- Graph form: one additive relation in the profile amendment, named by engram **`realizes_archi`** (snake_case, consistent with `has_field` / `in_workpackage`) `[FAIT: .tmp/rv/generic-appctx-engram.md:36]`, `Component|Route|ApiEndpoint|DataEntity|CodeSymbol --> ArchiRef`, where `ArchiRef` is a lightweight node carrying `{archiNodeKey, kind, form}` — the archi document itself stays outside the code-grain graph. Endpoint kinds are **profile data** checked by the validator, never hardcoded in extractors: `Route|Component` → ArchiRef kind `package|component`; `ApiEndpoint` → `api`; `CodeSymbol` → `runtime|service`; `DataEntity` → `store` **only**. Any other pairing yields a `bridge-kind-mismatch` diagnostic and no edge. Q8 decides only the amendment vehicle; the name and endpoint table are engram's and are settled here.
- Two bridges, never confused: `realizes_archi` (this section) joins **app-context code-grain nodes to archi system-grain nodes** at `archi@1` today. `archi2-erd-bridge@1` joins **erd entities to archi@2** (`storedIn` → `store`, `realizes` → `dataEntity`) `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/archi-card-semantics/BRIDGE-ARCHI-ERD.md:21]`. The app-context `DataEntity` (a physical table read from Drizzle) corresponds to the erd entity side of that target bridge, which is why it may realize a `store` and nothing else here; it is never equated with the conceptual archi@2 `dataEntity`.
- Filtering by architecture: the cluster-mesh component tools (§5.3) filter « UI only », « API only », etc. by joining the archi instance (kind/form) with these edges at the pinned commit — this is the mechanism behind « on va filtrer car on ne code que l'ui » while the architecture stays general. This architecture filter is a *view*; the *authorization* filter is the dev scope (§4–§5.2). v1 blurred the two; v2 keeps them separate named mechanisms that compose (a node is served only if it passes both when both are requested).

Alternative considered and not retained as primary: bridge as a registry-only record in cluster-mesh. It avoids a profile amendment but is not commit-pinned and cannot be traversed by local `engram serve`; retained only as fallback if engram refuses the profile amendment (Q8).

### 3.5 Cross-app instance registry: extension of the existing registration (cluster-mesh)

`[JUGEMENT]` **D-G4.** There is exactly one registration per app instance: the existing `AppArtifactsRegistration` `[FAIT: origin/main:api/src/services/artifacts/app-registration.ts:9]`, extended by one additive optional block. ### 4.1 Definitions — class (engram), request, effective scope, authority

**D-G5 — `DevScope` class (engram metamodel).** `[JUGEMENT]` `DevScope` is a session-time overlay typed by the `app-context@1` profile class. Each of its two permission sets (readable, modifiable) may carry three reference families, so every consultable category can be designated precisely (R3-5):

- **Code targets** — the frozen `TargetSelector` union (`component|route|endpoint|entity|field|symbol`) plus `ArchiRef` keys from the bridge (§3.4), unchanged. The frozen union is a *tool-input* type; DevScope reuses it, never extends it `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:391]`.
- **Documentation items** — `SpecContextRef`: a frozen `SpecSelector` (`path` and/or `specId`, optional `sectionSlug`, optional `requirementId`) `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:399]`, resolved at admission against the pinned-commit graph to exact `Spec`/`SpecSection`/`Requirement` node ids. Readability is per item and per text range (§5.2 rule 4): naming one section authorizes neither its neighbours nor its parent range.
- **Track items** — `TrackContextRef`: `{kind: "workpackage"|"item"|"decision", ref: <stable Track reference>}`, resolved to exact `TrackWorkpackage`/`TrackItem`/`TrackDecision` node ids. A readable Track element never makes its neighbours readable. Track references are **read-only context**: they never make Track journal files writable, and Track mutations keep Track's own command authority (§2.3).

The class also carries an optional **referenced-source pointer** (below) so an app whose write ceiling lives in its own repo is referenced, never copied. `DevScope` is never extracted from a repo and never a build-time graph node. Engram's part ends at the class shape and at applying a supplied instance (§5.2).

**Referenced scope source.** `ScopeSourceRef = {repo, path, commit, digest}` names, inside `EffectiveDevScopeV1.provenance`, the file whose declared allow/deny lists serve as one ceiling level: `repo` is the canonical repo key, `path` is repo-relative, `commit` is the full immutable commit the file was read at (for immo, the session's `contextCommit`), and `digest` is the `sha256` hex digest of the file bytes as read at that commit. **Single timing rule (R4-3): « Resolve the registry reference by static extraction at graph-build time, recording repo, path, full commit and file digest. Admission verifies that build resolution and intersects its ceiling; it refuses missing or unverifiable resolution »** `[FAIT: .tmp/rv/generic-appctx-hcond.md:56]`. The extraction — static reading of the declared literal lists from the file bytes at the build commit, performed at graph build (P1, §5.5) by the h2a-owned extractor (G-H3) — records a **build resolution** (`{repo, path, full commit, file digest}` plus the resolved write lists) with the graph build metadata for that commit; **the referenced code is never imported or executed** `[JUGEMENT]`. Admission performs **no extraction**: it only verifies that a build resolution exists for the pinned context and that the build used that same full commit (the session's `contextCommit`) and that same file digest, then intersects its ceiling and pins the tuple as `scopeSourceRef`; a missing build resolution, a build-time extraction failure, or any commit/digest mismatch refuses admission (§4.2) — no other extraction timing is defined anywhere in this spec. The resolved lists live only inside the build resolution record, the effective scope, and its retained `write` rule object (§4.3); they are never copied into this spec, into Track declarations, or into an independently maintained profile (R3-1). By default a referenced source constrains the **write** dimension only (its allow list is a write ceiling, its deny list is a write denial) and its retained record carries no `read` object, so it abstains on read (§4.3 step 2); it defines no read denial unless the admission policy explicitly maps it to reading as well.

**Canonical target reference.** One logical tuple identifies an application target everywhere (Track links, requests, manifests, audit): `(application, repo, ontologyProfile, target, revision)` — serialization to be settled with engram `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/h2a/tmp/h-cond/appctx-scope/NOTE-scope-sessions-wp.fr.md:298]`. `[JUGEMENT]` Working serialization, to be confirmed in lot G-T1/G-E1: `appctx:v1:<appInstanceId>/<repoKey>/<profile>/<kind>:<selector-digest>@<commit-prefix>`, where `selector-digest` digests the canonical reference — a `TargetSelector`, an `archi:<archiNodeKey>`, a `SpecContextRef`, or a `TrackContextRef`, each with its family in `<kind>` so the families can never collide. A reference describes what work *concerns*; it grants nothing.

**Track representation (description, never authority).** Workpackages and items reference application targets through existing item links with reserved `kind: app-context-target` and the canonical locator above `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/h2a/tmp/h-cond/appctx-scope/NOTE-scope-sessions-wp.fr.md:297]`. ### 4.3 Inheritance — normative resolution algorithm

**D-G10 — Intersection over the full ancestor chain, separately for read and write.** `[JUGEMENT]` (h-cond target rule, made normative here; Q3 confirms):

1. **Chain and item selection.** Start at `trackItemRef` if given, else at `trackWpRef` (stable references only, never display labels or derived `WP<n>` codes). Walk `parentId` to the root, collecting **every** WP and spec-phase met — intermediate sub-WPs and ancestors above the chosen WP included. The existing `wpRootId` (highest WP only) is explicitly not this walk `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/h2a/packages/track/src/report/rollup.ts:190]`. The chain, with each level's declaration revision, is frozen into the effective scope (§4.1). The selected item's explicit application-target selection is an additional narrowing input to each applicable dimension; the item's stable reference and revision are snapshotted even when it is not a WP/phase, and changing its selection, links, or parent invalidates the scope (step 8). Links remain descriptive and cannot grant permissions `[FAIT: .tmp/rv/generic-appctx-hcond.md:11]`.
2. **Contributing levels and their inputs.** The levels, in no privilege order (step 3 intersects them all), are: the admission-policy ceiling; any referenced scope source validated by that policy (§4.1 — write dimension only by default); each WP/spec-phase in the chain; the selected item's selection; the launch request; and, for delegated sessions, the delegator's effective scope. Each WP/phase contributes its inert `ScopeDecl {allowed, forbidden, conditional}` path globs `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/h2a/packages/track/src/model/item.ts:38]` and its declared application permissions (read/write target and context-item sets, §2.3) when present. Track's single `ScopeDecl {allowed, forbidden, conditional}` maps to **both** dimension objects (`read` and `write` receive the same `allowed`/`forbidden` lists, with each conditional string stamped into a `{glob, state}` entry); dimension-specific declarations, the policy ceiling, and the request may populate one dimension object without touching the other, and a referenced scope source populates a `write` object only — its record carries **no `read` object and therefore abstains on read** (§4.1). Track stores strings and never matches them; all matching happens in the resolver/enforcers below, against these retained records only. `scopeValidate` remains advisory display only and is never consulted by admission as a verdict `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/h2a/packages/track/src/read/scope-validate.ts:1]`. Every level's rules are retained verbatim in `pathRuleSources` (§4.1) — one record per level, separate `read`/`write` rule objects, each with optional `allowed`, `forbidden`, and `conditional: Array<{glob, state: resolved-true|resolved-false|unresolved}>`, the source reference + revision standing as provenance for every rule and stamped state; enforcers never re-resolve a condition live, so a scope can never widen itself after admission. The records serialize canonically with the manifest: **serialization → reload preserves every per-level verdict without consulting the live Track/policy declarations** (G-H3 round-trip evidence), and both runtime projections are generated from these same records — the guard consumes them directly, and the harness/`BRANCH.md` projection (§4.4 point 4) preserves the per-level `write` objects, never a flattened union of ancestor lists. The top-level `forbiddenReadGlobs`/`forbiddenWriteGlobs` and projected `pathGlobs` of §4.1 are display/readback summaries derived from these records; they never replace them as the replayable rule set.
3. **Per-level verdicts, denials first (normative).** Evaluate each contributing level separately, per dimension (read, write — creations use the write dimension). **A matching forbidden rule, or a matching condition that is false or unresolved, denies before any allow rule. Intersect the resulting per-level permissions; never union ancestor allow lists. An absent restriction inherits (the level abstains on that dimension), an explicitly empty allow set denies everything, and unmatched paths deny** `[FAIT: .tmp/rv/generic-appctx-hcond.md:9]`. Concretely, for one level, one dimension, and one concrete path, evaluated **in this order** against that level's rule object for the dimension (§4.1): (a) the level carries no rule object for that dimension (absent `read`/`write`) ⇒ **abstain** on that dimension; (b) path matches a `forbidden` glob of that object ⇒ **deny**; (c) path matches a `conditional` glob whose stamped state is `resolved-false` or `unresolved` ⇒ **deny** — checked before, and therefore overriding, any overlapping allow (R3-3); (d) **`allowed` is present and `allowed.length === 0` ⇒ deny — this explicit empty-set test runs after (b)–(c) and BEFORE any conditional is tested as an allow, so an explicitly empty allow set denies even a path matching a resolved-true conditional (R4-1)** `[FAIT: .tmp/rv/generic-appctx-hcond.md:51]`; (e) `allowed` is absent ⇒ **abstain** (no deny above, no allow granted by this level — a resolved-true conditional alone creates no allow at a level that declared no allow set); (f) `allowed` is present and non-empty: path matches an `allowed` glob or a `conditional` glob stamped `resolved-true` ⇒ **allow**, otherwise ⇒ **deny**. A path is effective-allowed in a dimension iff no level denies it and at least the policy ceiling and the request allow it; abstention never creates a right. No level ever adds a target, context item, or path an ancestor did not allow.
4. **Intersection.** Reference sets intersect as resolved node sets at `contextCommit`, separately per family: `EffectiveTargets.dim = PolicyCeiling.dim ∩ (∩ levels Targets_i.dim) ∩ ItemSelection.dim ∩ Request.dim`, and likewise for context items; delegated sessions additionally intersect with the delegator's effective sets — delegation never unions permissions. Path permissions are the step-3 function, not a glob list: `EffectivePath(dim, path) = AND over levels of Verdict_i(dim, path)`. An item may only narrow its WP's selection. A WP spanning several workspaces grants no implicit access to other repos: `repo` must equal the session workspace's repo, and references in other repos resolve to refusal. After intersection the write sets must be subsets of the read sets family by family (§4.2.2); Track context items exist only in the read family (D-G5).
5. **Targets first, paths second; creations judged per concrete path (no textual glob algebra).** Intersection is computed on **reference sets**. For files that exist at `contextCommit`, the resolver expands each surviving target/context item to its concrete files (via the commit-pinned graph) and evaluates each concrete file with the step-3 function; the resulting file lists are the `pathGlobs` projections used for display and for read marking. The modifiable list so produced, after the shared-file degradation of step 7, is the **final modifiable projection** — the exhaustive set of *existing* files this scope may ever modify or delete. For **future** files, no expansion is possible and none is faked: the effective creation right is the retained step-3 function itself. A requested `create` glob is a candidate area, stored in `creatable.pathGlobs` for display. **Per-operation predicates (normative; every enforcer of §4.4 applies exactly these, on concrete paths, before mutation — R4-5):** **modify/delete** of a path `p` is allowed iff `p` is in the final modifiable projection **or** `p` was created during this session through an allowed creation under the same `(scopeId, version)` (recorded by the enforcer in protected session state), **and** `p` is write-allowed by step 3 — for an existing file, broad path globs alone never suffice, so an existing component B inside an allowed tree but outside the selected targets is refused even when a creation area covers its path, and a shared file degraded to read-only by step 7 is refused for the same reason (it is outside the projection by construction); **create** of `p` is allowed iff `p` does **not** exist at judgement time (checked by the enforcer against the pinned-commit file set plus the session-created set), `p` is write-allowed by step 3, **and** `p` matches a candidate creation area — if `p` already exists, the operation is judged as a modify and the creation area is irrelevant, so no « create over » an unselected existing file is possible; **rename** `src → dst` is allowed iff **modify/delete allows `src`** **and** `dst` satisfies the create path conditions (write-allowed by step 3 and inside a candidate creation area) **and**, when `dst` already exists, **modify/delete also allows `dst`** (replacing a destination is a deletion of that destination) — both ends are judged before the move, and a refused rename performs no partial move. A session-created file keeps this status only under the same scope version; invalidation or supersession (§4.3 step 8) lapses it together with the scope, and it never acquires graph-target membership from it. Glob-against-glob inclusion is therefore never decided — neither at admission (beyond syntactic validity) nor later — because every decision is taken on one concrete path: with a synthetic fixture `write.allowed: ["app/**"]` at one level and `write.forbidden: ["app/private/**"]` at another, `app/private/new.ts` is refused at creation even though no such file existed at admission, while `app/src/new.ts` is allowed when every level allows it and a creation area covers it; in the same fixture, an *existing* `app/src/b.ts` realized only by an unselected component B is refused at modify even though both the globs and the creation area cover it, because it is outside the final modifiable projection. Verifying present files is never claimed to prove anything about future files. A newly created file belongs to no graph node until the graph is rebuilt at a new commit and the session is re-admitted against it; until then only its path verdict governs it, and MCP reads keep serving the admission commit (§5.3).
6. **Deny-first classification. D-G12.** Effective-scope classification is the step-3 per-level function. It is deliberately distinct from the legacy BRANCH.md precedence in `classifyPath`, where explicit allowed wins so a granted subtree can sit inside a broader forbidden tree `[FAIT: packages/harness/src/scope/scope-boundary.ts:41]`. Cases (ii) and (iii) are the round-3 additions `[FAIT: .tmp/rv/generic-appctx-hcond.md:9]`; cases (vi)–(viii) are the round-4 additions `[FAIT: .tmp/rv/generic-appctx-hcond.md:51]`.
7. **Shared files.** A file realized by several targets is modifiable only if the admission policy explicitly lists it as shared-modifiable for this scope; otherwise it degrades to read-only. No widening ever follows from an import edge alone `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/h2a/tmp/h-cond/appctx-scope/NOTE-scope-sessions-wp.fr.md:79]`. The same rule applies to a file created during the session once a later graph build realizes it under several targets; before that build, step 5 alone governs it.
8. **Snapshot, invalidation, revocation.** Resolution sources (Track chain revisions, item selection/link revisions, policy version, request digest, any `scopeSourceRef {repo, path, commit, digest}`, and the stamped conditional values) are frozen in the manifest at launch (Q4). Any parentage change, scope/link/permissions declaration change on a chain member or on the selected item, policy revision, or a policy change repointing the referenced scope source **invalidates** the snapshot: drift is detected at binding time and per §5.4 re-verification, and the scope yields refusals until re-admitted — invalidation can only shrink or block, never widen. A referenced source can change only with a new commit, hence only through a new graph build at that commit (fresh static extraction recording repo, path, full commit and file digest) plus re-admission verifying that new build resolution; the pinned digest makes any other drift a refusal, not a silent update. Any extension requires a new admission (new `scopeId` or version bump recorded in the manifest). Revocation by the admission authority takes effect before the next tool call (§5.4).

**Filtering + marking rules (normative, applied in this order).** Let `R` = readable node set and `M` ⊆ `R` = modifiable node set, derived from the effective scope by resolving its targets **and context items** (§4.1) against the requested graph at the requested commit (a scope whose pinned commit/profile does not match the request's `{repo, commit}` is refused at the route before dispatch — the scope authorizes one context revision, §4.1; there is no second rule for any tool, §5.3).

1. **Candidate construction is filtered.** Traversal (§5.1, frozen rules) expands only nodes in `R`: a relation never carries the walk into or through a non-readable node (a helper chain through a non-readable helper stops there; an endpoint reachable only through it is not included; a non-readable parent `Spec`, governing spec, or Track ancestor is never pulled in by `contains`/`specifies`/`in_workpackage`). Following a link never widens rights: a readable node linked to a non-readable neighbour contributes the neighbour in no form. If the primary target `T0`/`S0`/exact `id` is not in `R` — or is a mixed text range under rule 4 — dispatch returns the frozen `target-not-found`, byte-indistinguishable from genuine absence, so existence is not leaked. For `engram_targets_for_spec`, `S0` resolution honours the frozen `SpecSelector` narrowing first (`sectionSlug`/`requirementId` select that exact node); an unnarrowed `Spec` is judged as the whole-document range under rule 4.
2. **Marking.** Every surviving `ContextRecord` gains exactly one of `inScope: true` (node ∈ `M`) or `readOnly: true` (node ∈ `R ∖ M`) — the engram-frozen vocabulary: `readOnly` means *served because readable*, never a consolation mark on non-readable data. Track context items are always `readOnly` (D-G5).
3. **No side channels.** Filtered-out nodes contribute nothing anywhere: no totals, no `candidates` entries, no diagnostic about them, no stub, no counter, no excerpt. `target-ambiguous` lists only readable candidates (ambiguity solely among non-readable nodes collapses to `target-not-found`); diagnostics are served only when their subject is readable **and** every range they cite is clean under rule 4, with their own `candidates[]` filtered the same way; `candidatesTruncated` keeps its frozen budget meaning and never encodes a filtered count.
4. **Whole text ranges only (mixed specs).** Frozen spans nest: a `SpecSection` spans from its heading to the next heading of same or higher level, subsections included `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:250]`, a `Spec` spans the whole file, excerpts in `ContextRecord`s are sliced from those same spans `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:435]`, and `engram_read_spec_section` pages through the full span. Filtering nodes therefore does not filter bytes, so readability of a textual node is defined on its **entire frozen range**: a `Spec`, `SpecSection`, or `Requirement` is servable iff the node itself is in `R` *and* every descendant textual node is in `R` *and* no non-readable textual range of a node that is neither the node itself nor one of its ancestors lies inside its range — **exactly equal included** — or partially overlaps it (a partial overlap putting non-readable lines inside the requested range blocks exactly like a descendant). **Descendant and ancestor are taken from the transitive graph parent chain only (`contains` for `Spec`/`SpecSection` nesting, `specifies` for a `Requirement` under its `Spec`/`SpecSection` `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify-wt-appctx-l2/src/app-context/extract-spec.ts:1702]`), never inferred from range geometry, because geometry cannot separate the two relations at equality: a document whose sole heading sits on line 1 yields a `Spec` spanning `[1,N]` `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify-wt-appctx-l2/src/app-context/extract-spec.ts:1733]` and its `SpecSection` spanning the identical `[1,N]` `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify-wt-appctx-l2/src/app-context/extract-spec.ts:1749]`, `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify-wt-appctx-l2/src/app-context/extract-spec.ts:1772]`, joined by a `contains` edge from parent to section `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify-wt-appctx-l2/src/app-context/extract-spec.ts:1775]`. That equal-range descendant blocks its parent exactly like a strictly interior descendant (F6, R5-1). Conversely, an enclosing *or exactly equal* ancestor range is not a blocking overlap (R4-6, extended to equality by R5-1): an ancestor `Spec`/`SpecSection` outside `R` is merely non-returnable itself — it is not an interdiction over the lines of its descendants, so it never blocks a descendant whose own range is clean, even when the two ranges are identical — the same parent/descendant pair therefore refuses under the parent id and serves under the descendant id when only the descendant is in `R`; and a non-readable sibling range does not block either, since it shares no line with the requested range (the shared parent range is the ancestor case, already carved out).** « Clean under rule 4 » (rules 3 and 5, per-envelope table) means exactly this predicate applied to the cited/served range itself, ancestors disregarded whether enclosing or equal. The check runs on the full range **before** excerpting, slicing, or paginating, so no later page can disclose what the first page withheld. Without modifying any frozen range (no shortening, no redaction, no re-slicing), a mixed range is refused whole: its id behaves as `target-not-found`, its records are excluded from list envelopes (never served with a trimmed excerpt), and its text is never served. The asymmetry is deliberate: an individually readable leaf (a `Requirement`, a subsection with no non-readable descendant) remains servable even when its parent range is mixed **or its parent is simply outside `R`**, because serving the leaf's own clean range discloses no other line — the parent stays refused as a whole, and a restricted neighbouring section stays refused, while the leaf alone is served (the G-E3 round-4 scenario: only the leaf in `R`, parent out of `R` ⇒ leaf served, parent and restricted neighbour refused; the G-E3 round-5 scenarios replay both directions at exactly equal ranges on a sole-heading-on-line-1 document: parent in `R` with its equal-range section out of `R` ⇒ parent refused whole, section in `R` with its equal-range parent out of `R` ⇒ section served, parent refused). A spec is therefore never returned whole unless its entire content is authorized `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/h2a/tmp/h-cond/appctx-scope/NOTE-scope-sessions-wp.fr.md:168]`.
5. **Budget after filtering.** Filtering (rules 1, 3, 4) and marking (rule 2) happen **before** pagination and budget packing; marks and the `devScope` echo are inside the frozen full-envelope rule, and `usedTokens` is computed on the exact final wire bytes as frozen. Conséquence : (a) permet à un niveau bas d'effacer une interdiction posée plus haut ; (b) un item ou une session peut réduire, jamais dépasser l'autorité reçue. **Recommandation : (b), évaluée niveau par niveau, avec les règles : interdiction ou condition fausse/non résolue = refus avant toute autorisation, verdicts intersectés sans jamais fusionner les listes d'autorisation des ancêtres, règles retenues séparément en lecture et en écriture pour chaque source (une source qui ne contraint que l'écriture s'abstient en lecture), champ absent = héritage, liste explicitement vide = rien — testée avant toute condition vraie qui la recouvrirait —, chemin non couvert = refus.** Objection : un ancêtre trop strict peut bloquer un travail légitime jusqu'à sa correction — assumé, par nouvelle admission. Plus petite réponse : « Q3 = b ». Lot : G-H3.

**Q4 — Évolution du périmètre pendant une session.** Options : (a) recalcul continu à chaque changement Track ; (b) instantané figé au lancement, avec invalidation sur changement de parenté ou de déclaration, révocation effective avant l'appel suivant, extension uniquement par nouvelle admission. Conséquence : (a) peut élargir une session en cours sans acte d'admission ; (b) garde une session stable et traçable, au prix d'une réadmission pour élargir. **Recommandation : (b).** Objection : friction si les déclarations Track bougent souvent pendant le travail. Ce qui la renverserait : un usage réel où le périmètre doit suivre le backlog en continu sans réadmission. Plus petite réponse : « Q4 = b ». Lots : G-H2, G-H3.

**Q5 — Liaison entre h2a et cluster-mesh.** Options : (a) résolution en ligne : cluster-mesh interroge h2a pour rattacher connexion, session et périmètre, et revérifie à chaque appel ; (b) attestation autonome portée par la session, vérifiée sans appel ; (c) combinaison (attestation + cache borné avec invalidation). Conséquence : (a) est le plus simple à révoquer, dépend de la disponibilité de h2a ; (b/c) résistent mieux aux coupures mais compliquent la révocation immédiate. **Recommandation : (a) d'abord ; cache borné avec invalidation seulement si le besoin est démontré.** Objection : si h2a est injoignable, plus aucune lecture — c'est le comportement fail-closed voulu. Plus petite réponse : « Q5 = a ». Lots : G-H2, G-C2.

**Q6 — Consultation depuis le périmètre immo.** Options : (a) lire uniquement les cibles du périmètre immo admis ; (b) lire aussi les contrats explicitement liés à ces cibles et déclarés dans le profil. Conséquence : (a) peut cacher des contrats requis pour coder ; (b) n'ajoute que des contrats nommés en lecture, sans jamais ouvrir de droit d'écriture implicite sur les implémentations ni l'accès à tout le backend. **Recommandation h2a/track : (b).** L'éligibilité en écriture suit le plafond adossé au registre du §6.2.1, quelle que soit la réponse ici. Objection : la frontière « contrat nécessaire » peut dériver si les déclarations sont larges ; elle reste visible dans l'affichage avant lancement. Plus petite réponse : « Q6 = b ». Lots : G-I1, profils applicatifs.

**Q7 — Garantie locale sur les écritures.** Options : (a) consigne seule (le prompt dit le périmètre) — aucune garantie technique ; (b) garde de développement qualifiée (h2a refuse avant action toute modification, création ou tout renommage d'outil hors périmètre, par hôte qualifié) — ne résiste pas à l'exécution de programmes arbitraires ; (c) isolation système (bac builder : l'arbre protégé n'est jamais inscriptible directement par un programme, `exec`/`pty` inclus — les écritures vivent dans un espace jetable et seul un passage contrôlé juge chaque fichier, y compris la sélection finale des fichiers modifiables et les deux extrémités d'un renommage, avant toute mutation de la destination protégée ; tant que ce passage n'est pas qualifié, l'admission (c) est refusée) — la seule frontière résistante aux programmes arbitraires, formulée comme refus avant mutation de la destination protégée. **Recommandation : (b) pour le développement courant, (c) dès qu'une frontière résistante aux programmes arbitraires est exigée ; dans les deux cas, une session en écriture est refusée si le contrôle choisi n'est pas disponible.** Objection : (b) donne un sentiment de confinement supérieur à sa garantie réelle — la spec nomme donc le niveau dans chaque session et chaque affichage. Plus petite réponse : « Q7 = b (+ c pour …) » en nommant les cas visés par (c). Lots : G-H4, G-B1.

## 9. Pre-mortem

Assumption: twelve months later this design is judged a failure. Most plausible causes, each with the earliest observable signal and the mitigation already in the design or still missing.

1. **Read filter quietly bypassed by a side channel — including text bytes.** A new envelope field (counter, diagnostic candidate, stub, excerpt) ships unfiltered, or a range check is applied to node ids but not to the frozen text span those ids serve, and non-readable content leaks (the exact failure round 3 closed in §5.2 rule 4). Signal: G-E3/G-V1 fixture diffs — a non-readable witness string appears anywhere in any wire byte, in any excerpt or later page. Mitigation in design: rules 1/3/4 + the per-envelope table are normative for every response shape and every text-carrying field, and the witness-string suite asserts byte-level absence; residual gap = future engram fields added outside this amendment discipline.
2. **Admission authority confused with description — Track or referenced source.** A team « fixes » a refused session by editing Track declarations, or the referenced ceiling file in its own repo, from inside the session and expects rights to follow; or a resolver bug reads either input as authority (or worse, resolves the referenced file by executing tenant code, or re-extracts it at admission from bytes other than the graph-build ones). Signal: an effective scope whose `provenance.policyRef` is missing, whose Track revisions postdate `issuedAt`, or whose `scopeSourceRef {commit, digest}` does not equal the P1 build resolution attached to the served graph build. Mitigation: authority outside session-writable stores (§4.1, Q1), static extraction at graph-build time only with admission restricted to verifying that same full commit + digest, snapshot+invalidation (§4.3 step 8), provenance digests in every manifest and audit entry; a ceiling change helps only after landing in its repo, a new graph build (fresh extraction), and re-admission verifying it.
3. **Guard / builder overclaim.** A `guard-qualified` session is treated as system-isolated and an arbitrary program writes outside the tree (the guard's documented limit `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/h2a/docs/workspace-scope-guard.md:200]`); or a builder path publishes disposable-storage content without the checked apply gate, so a direct `exec`/`pty` write into a denied subpath inside an allowed tree reaches delivery (the exact failure round 4 closed in §4.4 point 3b). Signal: first point-4 evidence of a file the guard never saw, or a delivered diff entry with no apply-gate record. Mitigation: guarantee level is named in the manifest, the pre-launch display and `get_dev_scope`; the protected tree is never process-writable in the builder path and `system-isolation` admission is refused until the G-B1 direct `exec`/`pty` inside-tree qualification passes; Q7 decides which workloads require `system-isolation`; residual gap = human misreading of the level, never fully removable.
4. **Resolver availability becomes the outage.** Online resolution (Q5) makes h2a a hard dependency of every read; an h2a outage reads as a total app-context outage and teams route around the MCP. Signal: `session_not_admitted` rate in the audit sink during h2a incidents. Mitigation: fail-closed is the stated choice; the bounded-cache evolution is pre-named in Q5 without weakening revocation (cache entries re-verify on use, §5.2).
5. **Bridge rot.** `.engram/archi-bridge.yaml` drifts from the code; component views quietly under-report. Signal: `target-not-found` diagnostics at build. Mitigation: diagnostics are build-visible; missing piece = a CI gate failing on new bridge diagnostics (not priced; candidate add-on to G-V1).
6. **Profile hash churn.** Each additive amendment re-pins G0 and invalidates retained graphs (`profile-incompatible`). Signal: rebuild storms after G-E1. Mitigation: one bundled amendment (Q8); retention is only N=10 commits `[FAIT: /home/antoinefa/src/graphify/.graphify/scratch/design/app-context-graph/SPEC_EVOL_APP_CONTEXT_GRAPH.r4.md:194]`.
7. **Freshness friction.** Sessions pin a commit that was never built; `commit-not-indexed` becomes the most common answer. Signal: error-code distribution in the audit sink. Mitigation missing by design (never answer from another commit); the build-trigger owner is `unknown` (§7) and must be settled in G-C2 — on-demand build at the route with the retained cache is the working assumption, unpriced build latency is the risk. Note the new coupling: admission pins `contextCommit`, so a stale graph now blocks *launch*, not just answers — visible earlier, arguably better, definitely more annoying.
8. **Tenant/instance binding error.** A wrong `graphId` in one `appContext` block serves app A's graph to app B's tenant; engram cannot notice (it never decides tenancy), and the scope filter cannot either (targets resolve inside the wrong graph just as happily). Signal: none inside engram. Mitigation: registration writes stay on their existing gated, revisioned path (`reviewedBy`) `[FAIT: origin/main:api/src/services/artifacts/app-registration.ts:31]`; G-C1 tests assert cross-tenant refusal; residual risk = reviewed-operator error.
9. **Shared-file and creation-area friction.** Design-system-wide files (tokens, global styles, route tables) map to many targets and keep degrading to read-only (§4.3 step 7), and candidate creation areas drawn too narrow keep refusing legitimate new files one path at a time (§4.3 step 5); teams respond by requesting giant target sets and giant creation areas « to be able to work », and scopes widen socially. Signal: share of scopes carrying explicit shared-modifiable lists, median modifiable-target count per admission, and the rate of refused creations that are later re-admitted wider. Mitigation: shared-modifiable lists and creation areas are explicit and auditable per policy, and each future path is still judged individually; no structural fix — this is the cost of the no-implicit-widening rule, stated now so it is not discovered as a bug.
10. **`archi@2` migration breaks instance identity.** The target is no longer `unknown` — its kinds are specified (§3.1) — but adopting it still renames or adds kinds, so bridge keys and `archiNodeKey`s can stale across every app at once if migration is rushed. Signal: engram `@2` profile freeze / G0 registration. Mitigation: pin `archi@1` per registration (§3.5), author instance documents in dual-version form from day one (§6.2), and migrate per app by document revision, never as a flag day; the `storedIn`/`realizes` bridge stays a separate profile and is never merged into `realizes_archi` by renaming.
11. **Two sources of scope truth.** `BRANCH.md` (human-edited) and the manifest diverge; scope-check passes against a stale branch file while admission says otherwise. Signal: a `VerificationRun` whose marker digest differs from the manifest digest. Mitigation: `BRANCH.md` and the harness input are generated from the effective scope's retained per-dimension records with levels preserved (G-T2, G-S1), never hand-edited for admitted sessions and never flattened into merged lists; digest mismatch is itself a finding (§4.4 point 4).
12. **Engram lot slippage blocks Phase R.** G-C2 depends on the frozen L6 provider and now also on G-E3's filtering; the route contract already records the sequencing (« a cluster-mesh route wagon after engram L6 » `[FAIT: .tmp/rv/engram-route-contract.md:11]`). Mitigation: M-G1 (§7) is valuable and verifiable with engram + h2a alone; admission (G-H1…G-H3), registry (G-C1) and links (G-T1) proceed in parallel regardless — but no partial configuration may be *announced* as authorized reading until points 0–2 are jointly live.

## 10. Glossaire court (FR)

| Terme | Signification dans cette spec |
|---|---|
| **Admission** | L'acte de h2a qui, avant tout démarrage, résout le périmètre effectif d'une session et le consigne dans le manifeste protégé. Sans admission, aucune lecture. |
| **Cible** | Élément de code désigné par un sélecteur gelé (composant, écran, endpoint, entité, champ, symbole) ou par une clé d'architecture. |
| **Élément de contexte** | Section ou exigence de spec, ou élément Track (lot, item, décision), désignable précisément dans un périmètre, sans autoriser ses voisins. |
| **Périmètre effectif (`EffectiveDevScopeV1`)** | L'instantané résolu à l'admission : ce que la session peut lire, modifier et créer, avec sa provenance. C'est lui qui fait foi, pas la demande. |
| **`inScope` / `readOnly`** | Marques posées par engram sur chaque élément servi : modifiable, ou lisible sans être modifiable. Un élément non lisible n'apparaît sous aucune forme. |
| **Plage de texte mélangée** | Portion de spec dont l'étendue gelée contient, en son intérieur, au moins une ligne non autorisée. Elle est refusée entière ; on ne la tronque jamais. Une plage parente non autorisée qui englobe une feuille propre ne bloque pas cette feuille : la parente est refusée, la feuille autorisée peut être servie seule. |
| **Zone de création (`creatable`)** | Zone candidate où la session peut créer de nouveaux fichiers ; chaque chemin concret reste jugé au moment de l'écriture. |
| **Source référencée (`scopeSourceRef`)** | Pointeur `{repo, path, commit, digest}` vers le fichier qui porte un plafond (pour immo, son registre). On le référence, on ne recopie jamais ses listes. Il ne contraint que l'écriture et s'abstient donc en lecture. |
| **Résolution au build du graphe** | Moment unique de lecture d'une source référencée : extraction statique au build du graphe, avec dépôt, chemin, commit complet et empreinte consignés. L'admission vérifie seulement que le build a utilisé ce même commit et cette même empreinte ; elle n'extrait jamais. |
| **Projection finale des fichiers modifiables** | Liste exhaustive des fichiers existants au commit qu'une session peut modifier ou supprimer. Un fichier existant hors des cibles sélectionnées y est absent, même sous des motifs larges ou une zone de création qui le recouvre. |
| **Destination protégée / espace jetable** | Côté builder, l'arbre livré (destination protégée) n'est jamais inscriptible directement par un programme ; les écritures vivent dans un espace jetable de session et seul un passage contrôlé les reporte, après jugement, vers la destination protégée. |
| **Pont (`realizes_archi`)** | Lien déclaré entre le grain code (app-context) et le grain système (archi), assemblé au build du commit. Distinct du pont futur `storedIn`/`realizes` entre erd et archi@2. |
| **Vue vs autorisation** | La vue filtre l'affichage par couche d'architecture (par exemple l'interface) ; l'autorisation décide ce qui peut être lu ou écrit. La première n'accorde jamais rien. |
| **Manifeste protégé** | Enregistrement durable de la session hors des chemins inscriptibles par l'agent ; la présence n'en publie qu'une référence et un résumé. |
| **Phase R / Phase W** | R : consultation autorisée, sans promesse sur les écritures. W : sessions en écriture, admises seulement avec le contrôle qualifié choisi en Q7. |
| **Heures-agent** | Unité des estimations du §7. Aucune estimation n'est exprimée en semaines. |
